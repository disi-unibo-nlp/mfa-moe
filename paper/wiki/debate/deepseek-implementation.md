# Follow-up scientific critique

**Bottom line.** The design is now a credible prospective trial: within-prefix randomized mechanism runs, family-clustered ITT policy runs, matched-random controls, a positive-only intervention, and partial identification for missing labels. Its vulnerabilities are concentrated in five places: decision-time validity of nomination, cross-architecture fidelity of the dose and trigger, closure as a treatment-affected endpoint, reader/missingness identification, and failure to seal the reuse and statistical contracts before final treatment. These are fixable without adding arms.

## 1. Discovery nomination can select a target, but cannot establish anticipation

Checked-versus-unchecked prefix profiles can nominate a useful verification target only as an association-based selection heuristic. They cannot show that the model anticipates checking. The checked label is resolved after the prefix; sentence-scale labels and trailing routing features can encode the candidate’s later fate, problem type, position, or length. The packet already says token-scale anticipation is unavailable until raw prediction-row alignment and a prefix-only baseline are qualified. If those are absent, the profile is a post-hoc marker, not a precursor.

**Minimal fix.** Freeze the decision point and build features only from tokens at or before it. Select experts inside discovery families, evaluate out-of-family against matched null features (position, length, subject/level, candidate type, baseline exposure), and freeze the nominated expert before validation or final data. “Family-matched” must mean matching on pre-treatment family covariates, never final-family outcomes. The prospective targeted-versus-random ITT contrast, not the discovery profile’s fit, decides whether the nomination is useful. If no qualified prefix-only baseline exists, call it exploratory target nomination and make no anticipation claim. Also note the existing support constraint: the packet’s cheap proposals currently yield essentially no apparent support for unchecked-complete or checked-complete candidates. If that remains true, the qualification failure is itself a limit result; it is not permission to relax the ten-family/70% rules.

## 2. Treatment fidelity and trigger/dose matching across seven architectures

One positive raw-logit bias may not produce the target inclusion increment of 0.25 ±0.025 at each frozen prefix. Raw-logit scale, normalization, grouped routing, shared experts, and capacity differ across checkpoints. Native routes also varied across executions (mean expert-set turnover 0.1824), so calibration on discovery hidden states does not automatically transfer to final prefixes. A bias that adds the nominated expert may simultaneously displace other experts; native-k preservation does not make the treatment a pure single-expert activation. Random-pool tolerance may be infeasible; keep the stated rule that no admissible pool is a qualification failure.

**Minimal fix.** Capture, within execution and at the same hidden states, native and executed top-k IDs and selected weights at every pulse token. Report achieved inclusion increment and ID-aligned relative dispatched-weight displacement for both targeted and random arms. Qualify dose transfer on the 32 validation families, then freeze one calibrated bias per model and the random-pool constructor/draw. Define the treatment as “positive bias to the nominated expert under native-k routing,” not “activating one expert.” Freeze one target-independent trigger so both biases can be applied at the same frozen token/prefix; otherwise the arms compare different policies, not the expert bias. No-fire and missed-dose cases remain ITT observations.

## 3. Closure is an outcome, not a neutral stopping rule

The v4 exact native-token repeat gate failed, and sentence-scale closure proxies are censored, compete with state exits, and are not token-aligned. If online closure detection is unqualified, “stopping at native reasoning closure” is not executable. Worse, if treatment changes when closure occurs, truncating the reader window at closure conditions on a post-treatment variable and can bias the arm contrast.

**Minimal fix.** Qualify an operational, arm-blind closure detector on validation; if it fails, prespecify a fixed-horizon continuation endpoint and remove closure-conditioned claims. Use a fixed token window as the primary reader window, with observed closure as a secondary endpoint, or otherwise predefine a closure-triggered policy estimand that explicitly includes differential stopping. Log pulse start, pulse end, achieved dose, closure, and cap for every run. Never impute closure, and never pool 256/1,024/16,384 horizons. Bind the pulse to min(256 tokens, verified closure) with no overlapping pulses.

## 4. Reader agreement and missing executions limit what can be claimed

Reader-defined verification is not gold accuracy. Agreement is currently unknown; readers who see the continuation may infer arm from its content; symbolic cases may be judged inconsistently. Missing executions, no-fires, failed readers, and failed seeds can be arm-dependent. ITT includes these by design, but that makes the estimate a policy effect, not a pure routing-mechanism effect.

**Minimal fix.** Pre-register the rubric, two-reader assignment, and disagreement handling. Report raw and chance-corrected agreement and the disagreement matrix by arm and candidate type, with symbolic cases separate; do not invent a held-out precision cutoff. Convert disagreement and missing outcomes into outcome sets and arm-specific worst-case bounds. Handle seed-level missingness before averaging: a failed seed is a bound, not an omission. Keep final-answer accuracy a separate, offline, arm-blind gold-graded endpoint; readers must not grade correctness, and reader “verification” must not be pooled with final-answer outcomes. Report arm-specific no-fire/failure rates. Executed-only or dose-conditional analyses are descriptive, not causal mechanism, because conditioning on execution is post-treatment. Cost missingness has an unbounded upper bound. Degenerate intervals are uninformative, not precise nulls.

## 5. Freeze, reuse, and statistical binding must precede final treatment

Before any final-family treatment, freeze: model/tokenizer/serving revision; 32/96 family manifests; discovery families; prompts; seeds and caps; candidate proposal rule and candidate text, identical across arms; nomination features, window, matching, and tie-breaks; bias calibration and value; random pool and deterministic draw; trigger and exact prefix matcher; pulse length and closure rule; arm definitions; failure/no-fire handling; reader rubric, prompt, and blinding; accuracy grading; cost definition including controller output tokens and no-fire costs; estimands; the 42 contrasts; shared family resampling across models; seed aggregation with bounds; missingness handling; code hashes; and decision consequences. No final-family threshold tuning, pool relaxation, contrast selection, or model replacement.

Reuse original native source runs only when prompt, seed, sampler, model, cap, execution, and scoring bindings match exactly. They can be the Experiment 2 native arm, but they cannot be the Experiment 1 native continuation from a seed0 prefix, because that arm is prefix-conditioned. Experiment 1 and Experiment 2 estimate different things and must not be pooled. Freeze seed roles and pair the same seeds across arms within family/prefix; otherwise seed and arm are confounded. Instrument the actual online controller: the prior 16k engineering controller timer was zero and cannot price a semantic controller. Include controller output tokens, no-fire overhead, retries, and failed executions in policy cost; keep offline annotation, grading, capture, and storage separate.

## Mandatory versus optional

**Mandatory:** prefix-only decision-time nomination and out-of-family null comparison; per-architecture same-hidden-state dose fidelity and frozen random-pool admissibility; target-independent trigger alignment; closure qualification or a fixed-horizon endpoint; reader agreement and arm-specific worst-case missingness; separate gold accuracy grading; ITT primary; frozen candidate/arm/trigger/estimands; exact reuse bindings; controller cost; and reporting all seven models.

**Optional, using the same assigned runs:** hierarchical cross-model summaries, an arm-guess audit of readers, third-reader adjudication if disagreement is material, per-model forest plots, architecture descriptors, and cost–accuracy partial-frontier visualizations. No new sweeps, additional bias directions, narrower model scope, accuracy margin, positive-effect advancement gate, or retrospective threshold tuning is warranted.

An unsuccessful model should contribute a qualification-failure record if it fails a mandatory gate: exact gate, receipts, coverage, dose/admissibility/closure/agreement failures, and consumed cost. It should not be replaced or treated as a null. If it passes qualification but yields null, wide, or degenerate ITT estimates, it contributes those bounded per-model estimates to the shared family and heterogeneity analysis. If it fails after final treatment, it still contributes bounded ITT. All seven remain in the record; unsuccessful models are evidence about limits and applicability, not noise to be dropped.
