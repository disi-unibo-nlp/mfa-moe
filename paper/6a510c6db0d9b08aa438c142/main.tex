\begin{abstract}
Mixture-of-experts language models select a subset of feed-forward experts
for each token. We examine what these selections reveal about labelled
mathematical reasoning across seven checkpoints and six benchmarks.
On a common population of 522 questions in 509 problem families, routing
improves held-out reasoning-label prediction beyond a retrospective text
baseline in every model, reducing cross-entropy by 0.079--0.149 nats per
sentence. The original corrected intervals exclude zero, including under
a lexical-null comparison. A separate next-class analysis finds predictive
information beyond past text and history for GPT-OSS and Qwen3.6; its scope
is limited to those two checkpoints. We also compare expert keep-sets
learned on training families at 75\% retention per layer. Across five folds
covering 1,486 families, state-balanced selection improves minimum held-out
class coverage over labelled-frequency selection in five models; frequency
performs better in Qwen3.5 and Qwen3.6. Thus reasoning labels provide
information about expert allocation, while the benefit of balancing them
depends on the model. These results concern prediction and coverage of
saved native routes. Accuracy after expert removal and realized resident
memory savings remain unestablished.
\end{abstract}
