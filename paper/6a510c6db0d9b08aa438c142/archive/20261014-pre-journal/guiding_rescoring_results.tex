\subsection{Consistent offline answer-equivalence audit}
\label{sec:guiding-rescoring}

The qualitative inspection identified equivalent final answers rejected by
the historical normalized-string/numeric fallback. A uniform offline pass
therefore rescores both conditions in every saved, manifested identity and
margin evaluation: 13 complete full stochastic comparisons, two complete
greedy comparisons, the 39-problem Minerva comparison, and the interrupted
Qwen subset. It reads 40,097 stored condition records, including reused
baseline copies; this is not a count of independent generated solutions.
The original text, tokens, manifests, policies, and judgments are preserved.
Revised decisions and all changed answers are recorded separately in
\path{report/guiding_rescoring.json}.

The rule first extracts the last nonempty balanced boxed candidate, then
uses the existing answer-marker and last-number fallbacks when no such box
is present. Only that candidate is compared with the gold answer. It
normalizes display notation, plain-text labels, common units, currency,
and explicit list separators. Mathematical comparison uses Math-Verify
0.9.0 with latex2sympy2\_extended 1.11.0, ANTLR 4.13.2, and SymPy 1.14.0\footnote{\url{https://github.com/huggingface/Math-Verify}}.
Variables and structured objects use strict comparison; exact-number
answers require equality, while decimal/scientific scalar answers allow
relative tolerance $10^{-6}$ with no absolute floor. Thus zero does not
match a small nonzero answer merely because its magnitude is below
$10^{-6}$. Scientific notation is parsed numerically, rather than as a
symbolic expression involving $e$. Text denoting multiple answers is not
silently reduced to its first number. Standard unit annotations follow the
parser's normalization conventions; this is not a dimensional-analysis check.

The pass changes 4,054 stored judgments from incorrect to correct and one
from correct to incorrect. It also changes the extracted candidate in
2,065 records. The single downward change concerns a capped Qwen baseline
whose extracted box contains the literal placeholder ``answer'' rather
than its earlier saved numeric fallback. These counts include repeated
baseline copies. Changes occur on every benchmark, mainly OlympiadBench,
MATH-500, and Minerva. The analysis reruns the same 5,000 paired,
dataset-stratified source-problem bootstrap resamples (seed 42), including
the equal-problem sensitivity statistic. The 494-ID interrupted comparison
remains separate and its accuracy counts are unchanged.

No revised positive accuracy interval excludes zero in the complete
comparisons under either reported weighting. In particular, the earlier
positive secondary OSS findings and greedy Qwen margin interval do not
survive the common scoring rule. The Qwen concurrency-25 margin lower
endpoint is exactly zero. Strong negative effects under paper-style
promotion remain. Generation costs and termination flags are unchanged.

The checker is tested on equivalent and distinct fractions, text labels,
scientific notation, ordered answers, units, currency, multiple-answer text,
and very small numerical values, as well as final-candidate extraction.
It does not provide independent human adjudication. There are 699 records
with an unparsed candidate or reference, 332 with unsupported embedded
text, and 30 parser/comparison errors; these receive an incorrect decision
unless normalized display equality already succeeds. Error categories
and changed judgments remain available for further review. A two-second
parser/comparison timeout and a 2,048-character limit for nonnumeric
expressions bound degenerate inputs. Calibration and observational
correlation labels retain their original values: the revised evaluations
test the existing frozen policies, not policies refitted with revised labels.
