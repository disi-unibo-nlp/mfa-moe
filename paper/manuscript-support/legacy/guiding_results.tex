\section{Held-out Routing Interventions}
\label{sec:guiding}

\paragraph{Calibration and evaluation population.}
The original Qwen identity and margin policies split source problems within
each benchmark, allocating 1,082
problems to calibration and 465 to evaluation (70/30, seed 42).
Calibration uses 3,014 scored traces from 1,001 problems; 238 traces with
missing saved correctness are excluded. This differs from the correlation
analyzer, which counts known-gold missing answers as incorrect. The
problem-weighted calibration accuracy is 0.5817 on this scored subset.
Consequently, calibration targets may be sensitive to answer-extraction
failures and must not be interpreted as estimates over all allocated traces.
Each problem contributes total weight one, divided across its usable attempts.
Evaluation retains 150 MATH-500, 9 AIME24, 9 AIME25, 203 OlympiadBench,
12 AMC23, and 82 Minerva problems. With 32 attempts on each AIME/AMC
problem, this gives 1,395 paired attempts. No evaluation problem is used
in policy calibration.

\paragraph{Identity policy.}
At each of the six selected Qwen layers, expert inclusion is weighted by
its routing fraction and problem weight. Experts require support from at
least four distinct problems and positive accuracy lift over the calibration
baseline. At most eight identities per layer are selected; positive lifts
are normalized to the largest retained lift. A signed strength scales the
additive router-logit bias. Strength $+1$ promotes these identities;
strength $-1$ suppresses the same identities. This negative-strength control suppresses positive-lift experts; the additional
negative-polarity conditions below select a separate set of negative-lift experts. Unlike the
pooled-ID observational screen, policy targets are layer-qualified.

\paragraph{Margin policy.}
The policy calibrates the trace-mean selection-boundary margin
$p_{(k)}-p_{(k+1)}$, with $k=8$, using five problem-weighted quantile bins.
Among bins with at least four supporting problems, it selects the
highest-accuracy positive-lift bin, breaking ties by support and then lower
margin. At inference, probability mixing moves the token-level gap toward
the selected interval: uniform mixing reduces the gap, whereas mixing with
a distribution uniform over the selected top-$k$ increases it.
Trace-level calibration and token-level application have different units.
Both policies transfer routing targets from Unsloth 4-bit replay to GPTQ
generation, which is an additional unisolated deployment difference.

\paragraph{Matched evaluation.}
Each policy is compared with its own baseline using identical problem IDs,
prompts, prompt token IDs, per-attempt seeds, sampling settings, engine
configuration, and recorded software versions. Generation uses temperature
0.6, top-$p$ 0.95, and a 32,768-token limit. The original Qwen identity $+1$ and margin
comparisons use maximum concurrency 16; Qwen identity $-1$ and $+2$
use 25. The seven OSS identity conditions share concurrency 25 and the
same recorded generation contract. Baselines are interchangeable only
after compatibility and output-hash checks. Primary summary tables include
complete matched stochastic pairs. All ten complete identity comparisons
appear in Table~\ref{tab:identity-comparison}; the Qwen paper run was interrupted and its completed subset is reported
separately in Section~\ref{sec:partial-qwen-paper}.
Separate greedy and restricted-benchmark outputs are not pooled here.
The engine writes completed records incrementally and can resume without
continuing a partially generated sequence. Baseline reuse requires matching
contracts and a verified output hash; the Qwen and GPT-OSS margin runs at
concurrency 25 reuse compatible saved baselines. The evaluated controllers
use calibrated identities or current margins, without online episode
predictions. Annotation and probing support measurement and target exploration.
Accuracy tables use the uniform offline equivalence rule in
Section~\ref{sec:guiding-rescoring}; original judgments remain archived.
Calibration labels and fitted policies are held fixed.

\paragraph{Observed effects and limits.}
Qwen identity $+1$ changes 80 recorded outcomes and loses four net correct
answers; margin guiding changes 79 and gains one. Identity $-1$ changes
77 and gains 15. The corresponding rescored changes are $-0.29$,
$+0.07$, and $+1.08$ points. These original three comparisons do not
establish a beneficial direction. The negative-strength point estimate is positive, but differences across
strengths are not a controlled ranking because their baselines and
concurrency differ. These are exploratory comparisons without a separate strength-selection set.
Paired, dataset-stratified source-problem bootstrap intervals use 5,000
resamples with seed 42. All attempts and both conditions remain together
when a problem is sampled; pooled accuracy retains its attempt weighting.
The 95\% intervals for identity $+1$, identity $-1$, and margin guiding
are respectively $[-1.15,+0.65]$, $[-0.14,+2.37]$, and
$[-0.86,+1.01]$ percentage points. All include zero, so these comparisons
do not establish the direction of the accuracy effect. They do not establish
equivalence either. Each AIME evaluation has only nine source problems,
which limits the precision of benchmark-specific bootstrap estimates.
Intervals are conditional on the frozen policies and saved attempts, and
are not adjusted for multiple comparisons.
Pooled attempt accuracy also weights each AIME/AMC problem 32 times as
heavily as each single-attempt problem; benchmark counts are reported to
make this weighting explicit.

\paragraph{Completed identity comparisons.}
For Qwen fixed positive-polarity guiding, strength $+1$ changes accuracy
by $-0.29$ points with interval $[-1.15,+0.65]$, and strength $-1$ gives
$+1.08$ with $[-0.14,+2.37]$. Increasing promotion to strength $+2$
gives $-2.80$ points with $[-5.16,-0.72]$. Thus stronger promotion
reduces primary attempt-weighted accuracy under the revised scorer.

All seven GPT-OSS identity conditions use four targets at each of layers
15, 19, 21, and 23, matching native routing top-$k=4$. Each evaluates
1,395 matched attempts from 465 held-out problems at concurrency 25,
with a frozen chat-template date of 22 September 2026. Rescored baseline
accuracy is 77.85\% (1,086/1,395) in every condition.
Fixed positive strengths $+1$, $+2$, and $-1$ give changes of $0.00$,
$-3.30$, and $-1.00$ points. Only the strength-$+2$ interval excludes
zero: $[-6.16,-0.93]$. Fixed negative-polarity promotion gives $+0.57$
points with $[-1.58,+2.65]$ and does not establish a benefit.

Paper-style promotion at strength $+1$ reduces accuracy to 16.20\%
(226/1,395) for positive targets and 18.28\% (255/1,395) for negative
targets. Changes are $-61.65$ points with $[-69.32,-53.41]$ and
$-59.57$ with $[-67.24,-51.40]$, respectively. Mean generated length
rises from 6,497 to 22,489 and 23,639 tokens; token-limit hits increase
from 74 to 670 and 815. Suppressing the positive set with paper-style
strength $-1$ gives 75.63\% accuracy and a smaller but below-zero
primary change of $-2.22$ points ($[-4.16,-0.43]$); mean length is
6,698 tokens with 106 limit hits.

Paper-style steering adapts the extreme-score intervention with
$\epsilon=0.01$ while retaining our accuracy-lift selector
\cite{fayyaz2025steermoe}; it is not a full reproduction of SteerMoE.
The four-target budget does not prevent severe degradation from promotion.
Promoting a full top-$k$ set fixes membership and equalises its hook scores;
this is a possible mechanism, not a verified explanation of termination.

\paragraph{What the margin hook actually changes.}
Full diagnostics for the completed margin comparison record 20,657,039
hook token evaluations per selected layer. The top-eight identity set changes
on 32.7--41.8\% of these evaluations. Mean distance to the target interval
falls from $2.19$--$4.71\times10^{-4}$ to
$0.285$--$0.902\times10^{-4}$ across layers. Thus the numerical operation
moves the intended gap, but it does not isolate a membership-preserving
intervention after finite-precision log-probability conversion and casting.
These diagnostics describe the hooked logits, not an independent recording
of the kernel's dispatched expert IDs. Their denominator can include
prefill, padding, and recomputation and is not a generated-token count.
The identity $-1$ comparison uses minimal diagnostics, so detailed hook
statistics are not available on a common basis across all conditions.


\paragraph{Separate generation protocols.}
The separate greedy comparisons use 465 attempts on 465 problems.
Qwen identity guiding gives $-0.22$ points ($[-2.58,+2.15]$), and
margin guiding gives $+0.86$ ($[-1.29,+3.01]$). The 39-problem
Minerva-only identity comparison changes from 24 to 25 correct answers,
or $+2.56$ points ($[-5.13,+12.82]$). All three intervals contain zero.
These distinct decoding protocols and populations remain separate from
the full stochastic comparisons.
