## Direct answers to the seven challenges

**1. Is A2 a current defect or a future gate?** It is a **future gate**, not a current pipeline defect. The v3 price is pre-generation by design, no deactivation generation or rating job was submitted, and the intended later price is supposed to be newly constructed and post-generation hash-bound. The schema mismatch is a latent integration risk that becomes a defect only if the later v4 price is not built to the driver contract. The audit’s action should move from “repair a blocked current stage” to “build and preflight the future price before deactivation rating.” The 1,024-token reader cap does **not** satisfy A1’s 1,024-token trajectory endpoint.

**2. Does the post-hoc selection concern remain?** Yes, in modified form. Exact full-row manifest validation fixes a different problem: it ensures the held manifest binds the intended rows and prevents silent substitution. It does **not** fix how the 13 families entered the pool or the conditioning on high detector/rater agreement. A pilot effect is estimable in the **selected 13-family/start population, conditional on selection**, ideally stratified into the 8 candidate-to-verify and 5 approach-to-commit families; precision for the latter is very low. It is not an estimate for the registered 48-family discovery population or the broader detector-positive population without re-enrollment or pre-specified sampling. The untouched validation/utility pools can support later generalization, but not as a retrofit of this pilot.

**3. Does native nondeterminism support an exploratory randomized ITT or block generation until exact top-k determinism?** It supports a **distributional exploratory ITT** in principle, not an exact-replay or exact-paired-native contrast. The native duplicate is a draw from the native distribution; divergence in unordered top-k and continuation text means the native arm is not a deterministic counterfactual. Exact native top-k determinism is therefore not a necessary precondition for a randomized ITT that treats native as repeated draws, but it **is** necessary for claims about an identical native continuation or exact paired difference. Minimal additional control: run at least two native duplicates per start, interleave them with intervention arms in pre-registered randomized/counterbalanced order, record order, and include native-duplicate variance in the analysis. If the intervention is applied only after a shared prefix and divergence occurs before that point, then shared-state assumptions fail and either block or branch from frozen states. The held generation should remain blocked for now because of fixed ordering, not because exact top-k determinism is absent.

**4. Does A1 apply to the pilot, the paper objective, or both?** It applies to the **paper objective** and to any ordered-trajectory claim made from the pilot. It does not invalidate the pilot as a 256-token immediate-step exploratory screen. The pilot can estimate first-step semantic effects; it cannot estimate ordered transitions, dwell, re-entry, loops, reversal order, or the 1,024-token endpoint. Independent untouched validation/utility pools do not change that; they are later-stage resources.

**5. What blocks the fixed-prefix pilot versus later online/ordered work?**  
*Fixed-prefix pilot blockers/conditions:* If prefixes are already frozen and provenance-clean, A4’s live-gate defect does not block it; if they were selected via v2.1, revalidate through v2.3. A9/A11 matter only if the random-control schedule is used and interpreted as a pre-registered fixed control; otherwise record and model it. Fixed-order execution blocks causal release unless order is randomized or counterbalanced. A8 requires per-transition prompt-sensitivity handling, not arm unblinding. A1, A2, A6, A7, and A10 are claim-scope/later-stage matters for the pilot.  
*Later online/ordered blockers:* A4 version binding and v2.3 detector use; A1’s 1,024-token dense-label `trajectory_completion` run; A2’s post-generation price and CPU preflight; A3’s positive/negative/force-off design; A6’s parser, grader, and full cost ledger; A9/A11 exact control-schedule identity; A12 worker/dictionary bindings; A10 same-profile utility run.

**6. Is pairing plus duplicate-native enough for causal identification, or must order be randomized/counterbalanced?** Pairing controls between-start variation; duplicate-native measures same-arm native stochasticity. Neither makes arm assignment exchangeable with execution time. Fixed arm order within family/seed makes arm perfectly collinear with period and therefore vulnerable to secular drift—GPU state, load, warm-up, cache, thermal behavior, and host interference. Randomize or counterbalance before causal release: block-randomize arm order within family/seed, or use an ABBA/counterbalanced schedule across pairs, record order, model period effects, and retain multiple duplicates. With eight pairs, counterbalancing is safer than pure randomization. If the run already executed in fixed order, additional reversed-order replicates can diagnose order effects but may not fully recover the contrast.

**7. Does supplying the transition violate arm blinding?** No. Arm blinding hides whether the generation came from native, positive-bias, random, or negative-bias/force-off. Supplying the transition tells the rater which semantic criterion to apply, which is necessary when the rubric has two different criteria. The real concern is hypothesis priming/prompt sensitivity. A8 should be reclassified accordingly: keep arm labels hidden, pre-register the transition-specific rubric, report agreement separately per transition, and later audit paraphrased-rubric and rater-order sensitivity. A hypothesis-blind rating variant is a useful sensitivity analysis, not a substitute for telling the rater what property to judge.

## What the new facts do not change

- **A1 remains true for the paper objective:** the 256-token pilot cannot support ordered-trajectory, dwell, loop, re-entry, or reversal-order claims. The reader cap is not the trajectory endpoint.
- **A3 remains true as an interpretation requirement:** deactivation/negative bias answers necessity, not steering. A positive/negative pair and a proper force-off arm are still needed for a causal steering claim.
- **A4 remains operationally true:** patched v2.3 does not fix live gate v1 if v1 still imports v2.1. The live/online path remains version-unbound and the display-math/containment defect remains live there.
- **A5 remains true for generalization:** exact manifest validation is not selection-bias correction. The chosen 13 families support a conditional pilot estimate only.
- **A6 remains true for utility/accuracy:** no parser, grader, or full cost schema is evidenced. Accuracy and token-cost claims remain unestablished.
- **A9/A11 and fixed-order execution remain:** distinct random sets are not a frozen random-set identity, and neither is a substitute for exchangeability of arm order.
- **The overall verdict stands:** the packet is audit-friendly and contains real strengths, but it cannot yet answer the ordered-trajectory objective or the utility/accuracy objective.

## Shortest decisive experiment

For the **paper-level causal ambiguity**, the shortest decisive experiment is a pre-registered, randomized/counterbalanced, multi-duplicate **1,024-token dense-label trajectory run** on the retained pilot families, with positive and negative bias arms plus a force-off arm against native and duplicate-native controls, dose telemetry, and `trajectory_completion` as the ordered outcome. If resources are tight, run it first on the 13-family pilot and use the untouched mechanism pool only if it shows signal. For the **immediate causal ambiguity in the held 256-token screen**, the shorter experiment is simply to add randomized/counterbalanced arm order and at least two native duplicates per start to the existing immediate-step screen. The latter can resolve whether the pilot has any defensible immediate-step ITT; it cannot resolve ordered trajectory steering.
