\section{Analysis}

\subsection{Conclusions supported so far}

\begin{enumerate}
\item The static-reward, context-aware GEPA study reaches 0.650 cross-validated
macro-F1 with the light budget.  The advantage over balanced-accuracy
selection is small and the heavy search is worse.  In the separate completed
same-model LLM-judge final fit, development-set balanced accuracy rises from
0.677 to 0.777 and macro-F1 from 0.613 to 0.727, primarily through reduced
Monitor overprediction.  This establishes successful prompt optimization on
the development distribution, not held-out generalization.  The final
Qwen3.8-27B alignment does not reproduce that gain: balanced accuracy falls
from 0.740 for the imported seed to 0.729 for the GEPA candidate, and the
prespecified recall gate correctly retains the seed.
\item The boundary probes show substantial sentence-split decodability of
upcoming Schoenfeld episodes, with selected-layer AUROC of 0.835--0.934 and
the strongest thresholded performance for Implement, Verify, and Read.  This
establishes accessible prefix-state signal within familiar responses, not
generalization to unseen reasoning traces.  External application is best
interpreted as coarse phase inspection: a provisional pragmatic review gives
50.3\% strict and 60.8\% compatible agreement, with 187/581 multi-label units.
Implement is strongest (F1 0.729), while the remaining labels are incomplete
external tags.  ProcessBench Verify predictions identify review-heavy
post-error passages but do not identify correctness.
\item The September 10 vLLM correlation analysis completes 4,647 attempts
from 1,547 problems on six math benchmarks. All-attempt accuracy is
$3,588/4,647=0.772$, with 371 unparseable answers counted as incorrect.
Confidence has positive correctness associations with pointwise intervals
excluding zero on MATH-500, both AIME sets, and OlympiadBench; AMC and
Minerva remain uncertain. The 394 token-limit hits account for 393 of 1,059
errors. Among non-limit traces, confidence remains moderately positive on
AIME ($0.233$ and $0.207$), is near zero on MATH-500, OlympiadBench, and
Minerva, and is negative on AMC ($-0.352$). Complete-problem confidence
associations survive BH adjustment on both AIME datasets but not AMC.
Correct attempts within mixed-outcome problems have higher confidence yet
are 8,251--16,563 tokens shorter. New fixed-position views show early negative
and later positive confidence coefficients on AIME and AMC, while coverage
shrinks. Expert-rate maxima remain exploratory pooled-ID candidates.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What has not been established}

Neither GEPA final fit provides an unbiased test estimate.  The earlier run selects
among 145 candidates on the same six-response validation set, has only one
optimization seed, and leaves the locked 336-unit test unevaluated.  The result
does not establish that the expanded 40-demonstration prompt transfers to new
responses or that the same-model reward is an independent evaluator.  The
Qwen3.8 alignment reuses that selected prompt as its seed, optimizes and
selects on the same validation partition, and also leaves the locked test
untouched; retaining the seed is evidence that this search found no safe
development-set improvement, not evidence of generalization.

The boundary-probe run does not establish response-grouped generalization,
calibrated seven-way episode classification, or the cognitive states of Qwen's
own generations.  It uses Qwen to encode teacher-forced DeepSeek-R1 text,
shares all 38 response IDs between train and test, and selects layers on the
reported test set.  Its external argmax outputs have no authoritative human or
benchmark episode ground truth.  Agreement with the provisional hybrid review
is diagnostic and should not be called validated transfer accuracy.

The correlation results do not establish a dataset-invariant routing
predictor or a causal mechanism. No length-conditioned or dataset-adjusted
model has been fit, and the 32,768-token budget censors 8.5\% of traces.
Repeated-attempt comparisons control question identity but not length,
termination, or token content. The current run excludes GPQA-Diamond and
MMLU-Pro. Raw pooled coefficients mix datasets with different accuracy,
length, and routing baselines. Non-limit sensitivity checks select on
termination; late-position views select on reaching the window. Neither is
an unbiased correction for these confounds. The untagged views contain no
Schoenfeld class evidence. Layerwise and expert maxima are selected on the
analyzed data, and numeric expert IDs are pooled across distinct layer-local
modules. Family-specific BH adjustment does not supply held-out validation
or correct selection across position views. Across all three workflows,
correlation and linear decodability should not be read as causal evidence.

\section{Discussion}

The completed GEPA final fit demonstrates that same-model informative feedback
can guide a large prompt toward the development labels: it fixes 49 seed
errors while introducing 14 regressions and improves every validation
response.  Its optimization trace also shows the central risk.  The prompt
expands from 21 to 40 demonstrations and is selected after 145 candidate
evaluations on those same six responses.  The next defensible test is therefore
to freeze and ablate or compress the prompt, quantify response-level
uncertainty, and evaluate it once on genuinely unseen responses.

The Qwen3.8 alignment is an important negative result.  Although its internal
same-model reward increases, exact corpus-label metrics decline and Plan
recall breaches the prespecified 0.10 safety threshold.  Its shorter optimized
instruction also contains conflicting guidance for phrases such as ``Let me
compute that,'' plausibly contributing to more Plan-to-Monitor errors.  The
safety gate therefore performs its intended role: optimization reward alone
is insufficient evidence for deployment, and the byte-identical selected seed
should now be frozen rather than optimized further on this validation set.

The episode probes add a complementary representation-level result: the
prefix before a sentence often encodes the phase likely to follow, and the
signal is strongest in middle-to-late layers rather than at the final model
output.  The current split cannot determine whether this reflects a portable
episode representation, trace-specific transition regularities, position, or
formatting.  A response-grouped nested evaluation should select both layer
and regularization on inner folds, evaluate once on unseen responses, repeat
over seeds, and report response-bootstrap intervals.  Standardized or
norm-matched features and simple position, previous-unit, and formatting
baselines are needed to separate hidden-state information from layer scale and
trace structure.

For external use, the probe outputs should be treated as abstaining phase
suggestions rather than annotations.  Abbreviation- and LaTeX-aware
segmentation should precede evaluation; independent one-versus-rest scores
should either be calibrated on a common multiclass validation set or retain
multiple labels.  The pragmatic review confirms substantial ambiguity and
only 60.8\% compatible agreement.  Independent human annotators should resolve
the 37 low-confidence units and assess inter-annotator agreement before any
transfer-accuracy claim.  Cross-model recognition, as studied here, should
also be separated from Qwen's own generation by running the protocol on
Qwen-generated traces.

The new replay narrows the correlation interpretation. Negative length
associations recur across all six datasets, but supported positive confidence
associations occur in four, and the non-limit check leaves moderate positive
coefficients only on AIME. AMC has a negative non-limit confidence coefficient,
a positive within-question difference, and an uncertain negative
problem-level coefficient. These results concern different populations and
aggregation levels. Fixed-position windows further reveal early negative
and later positive coefficients while progressively excluding shorter traces.
A whole-trace confidence mean therefore cannot be interpreted as a uniform
signal throughout reasoning.

The next analysis should prespecify treatment of budget failures, jointly
examine length, termination, token composition, and dataset, compare windows
on common cohorts, and validate selected predictors on held-out problems.
Simply discarding limited traces or comparing late windows to early windows
with different membership does not resolve selection bias. Layer-qualified
expert rates are needed before the pooled-ID screen can motivate a particular
expert intervention. GPQA-Diamond and MMLU-Pro require separate execution
under the current generation/replay configuration to extend coverage.

\section{Conclusion}

The three active workflows support deliberately limited claims.  GEPA improves
the earlier same-model-judge development result substantially, but the final
Qwen3.8 alignment finds no safe improvement over that imported prompt and
neither run has been tested on an untouched response set.  The boundary probes reveal strong within-split
linear access to upcoming episode labels, especially for Implement, Verify,
and Read, but their sentence split leaks every response across train and test
and their external outputs collapse toward a coarse setup, execution, and
review taxonomy. The six-benchmark correlation study supports AIME
confidence/margin associations at both trace and problem levels, but the
results vary with benchmark, termination, aggregation, and token position.
Token-limit failures remain substantial, and later position windows select
longer traces. Expanded metric-pair, expert-rate, and reasoning-view screens
provide hypotheses for controlled evaluation without establishing an
independent or causal routing mechanism.

The immediate evidential requirements are a frozen-prompt GEPA evaluation on
unseen responses, response-grouped nested boundary probes with independently
validated external labels, a prespecified response to remaining generation
censoring, extension of the current run to GPQA-Diamond and MMLU-Pro, and
length-/termination-aware and common-cohort position analyses with
problem-respecting validation before routing interventions are evaluated.
