# Seven-model evidence inventory, 2026-10-08

This is a source-reuse audit, not a new experiment plan. `build_inventory.py` reads saved outcome summaries, the compact 68,129-row outcome sidecar, small cohort identity tables, and saved readout/profile results. It performs no model fit, bootstrap, inference, raw-capture processing or scheduler operation. `SOURCES.json` binds the inputs. The manuscript exhibit is `paper/6a510c6db0d9b08aa438c142/generated/model_analysis_review/benchmark_readout_tables.tex`.

Each complete historical/card population contains the same 1,547 problems per model: AIME24 30, AIME25 30 and AMC23 40 with 32 samples each, plus MATH500 500, Minerva 272 and Olympiad 675 with one sample each. Original 1,000-replicate question-bootstrap intervals are copied unchanged. These are per-cell intervals, not simultaneous model-comparison intervals.

The card-profile population has 33 unresolved adjudicated outcomes. Means exclude missing outcomes; the export includes separate strict/adjudicated scored denominators, unknown counts and simple all-assignment worst/best completion bounds. Those bounds are not confidence intervals. The historical accepted J1 deployment passed its own 98/100 audit; it is a different frozen source from the current verification campaign's 97/100 J1 failure. ## Existing positive analysis and populations

All seven saved CLEAN judge-context readouts improve present-class log loss; all model-specific saved seven-comparison CI7 intervals and lexical-null-excess CI7 intervals exclude zero. `routing_readout.csv` exports the source values directly. The original driver computes CI7 from each model's bootstrap distribution at quantiles 0.025/7 and 1-0.025/7; this audit performs no new joint across-model test or shared-question resampling. Every model has 522 questions/509 duplicate families, with 31,902--38,931 labelled sentences. Nemotron's exact interval is [-0.15628287233129554, -0.11819183955329762]; the proposed -0.11619 upper endpoint was a transcription error. This is held-out prediction of automatically labelled visible reasoning episodes, not latent cognition or a deployment benefit.

GPT and Qwen3.6 already have next-class and next-contained-class anticipation, exported in `anticipation.csv`. The other five models do not have those completed anticipation cells in the inspected source. Present-class readout across seven models must not be reduced to the two-model anticipation scope, nor may two-model anticipation be presented as seven-model evidence.

The September 29 `v3_analysis/results-r2/cross-4/` analysis contains all 14 A/B roots, superseding the older six-model implementation note's result summary. Cohort A has one outcome-blind sampled attempt for every one of 1,547 shared questions in each model. It is not another benchmark-accuracy sample. Saved cross-model meta-analysis treats model effects as pooling units and is not a new joint resampling of their shared question identities.

## Expert and transition results already available

`expert_profile_inventory.csv` binds all seven outcome-blind class-expert selections and corresponding per-attempt count archives. `expert_usage_correctness_saved.csv` preserves 147 layer-mean association rows after length, difficulty and class-token-fraction adjustment. Qwen3.5/Qwen3.6 Analyze-profile usage has small positive correctness associations (r=0.09179/0.08183); Verify-profile usage has negative associations (r=-0.09724/-0.10079). Four models have no q<0.05 mean-use row under this adjustment. These are same-cohort descriptive associations, not train-only expert keep-set validation or effects of deleting the nominated experts.

The compact enrichment files are `v3_analysis/results-r2/<model>/A/E_expert_enrichment.csv.gz`. Their `rate_in` is a **selected-slot share**, computed in `v3an/analyses.py:265,280` as count/(class_tokens*native_k), with no rate smoothing. Multiplying by `class_tokens*native_k` reconstructs counts. Summing the seven classes covers labelled tokens only. Full `class_counts.npz` contains per-attempt `counts[N,9,L,E]`, `tokens[N,9]`, `attempt_id`, and classes `Read, Analyze, Plan, Implement, Explore, Verify, Monitor, ALL_REASONING, ALL_LABELLED`. The latter permits future train-only aggregation without new inference, but was not processed across all seven models in this audit.

Stability evidence is not wholly absent. Qwen3.6's existing `steering-v1/runs/routing-control-v1/dense-verify-source-v2/RESULT.json` has 48 discovery families and 483 within-family/source-class matched Verify-entry positives. Four train-selected fold proposals have positive held-out directions. Fixed full-discovery L28/E189 and E9 differences are +0.08118 and +0.06296; these final identities do not have independent confirmation merely because fold-selected proposals replicate. `report/routing-study-v1/CANDIDATE_TABLE.json` additionally retains family-support and fold-direction information for 143,360 selection rows and the same number of executed-weight rows. That narrower Qwen study is not an all-seven deletion ranking study.

Correctness prediction is also not wholly absent. `report/experimental-resume-v1/R3E_B4_PRECISION_READOUT_v1.md` reports the completed GPT nested family-CV B4 analysis, its confirm-connected-family sensitivity and 1,000 simulated-outcome precision study. It has 508 scored questions/495 families and uncertain incremental predictive gain. Repeating that same experiment is not a missing contribution. The genuinely different unresolved question is whether a declared expert-retention ranking learned only on training questions generalizes to held-out questions and improves the accuracy--resident-memory tradeoff at matched layer quotas and bytes.

Old three-model transition matrices, Qwen3.6's 48 native boundary traces/42 transition profiles, and completed dense historical trajectories retain their original scopes. All-seven A labels and route statistics exist; a seven-model held-out transition/deletion-policy analysis was not found in the inspected inventory. Missing original sentence labels must break adjacency when forming transitions. For a static keep-set, retrospective **training** labels and training correctness are legitimate declared features; prefix-only features are required for a proposed online state-dependent intervention, and held-out outcomes must never select its policy.

## Static memory accounting and coverage review

`CHECKPOINT_HEADER_INVENTORY.json`, `checkpoint_tensor_bytes.csv` and `checkpoint_expert_tensor_bytes.csv` read checkpoint indexes, configs and safetensors JSON headers only. They include no weight payload or whole-weight hash. All expert names and native layer/expert counts match each indexed checkpoint; draft experts are separated. Native expert tensor bytes range from 10.166 GB (GPT) to 57.982 GB (Qwen3-30B), including expert-local quantization metadata. The GLM index declares 31.221 GB, while actual header data-offset ranges sum to 62.443 GB of tensors; both values and their difference are preserved. Header bytes are a better static accounting source than that index declaration, but neither is actual resident GPU memory.

The parent's `LABELLED_EXPERT_COVERAGE-r1.json` and `scripts/paper/export_labelled_expert_coverage_r1.py` were independently reviewed. All seven source hashes and script hash match; native-k mass, complete state/layer/expert frames, monotonicity and complete-set coverage checks are appropriate. An independent integer-count reconstruction for GPT and GLM exactly reproduced their half-keep coverage: GPT 88.3387% overall/84.5836% Monitor; GLM 71.9211% overall/59.5182% Monitor. Across seven checkpoints, keeping half the identities in every layer by observed labelled frequency covers 61.9045--96.1056% of observed labelled selections. Monitor is least covered for six models; Gemma's least-covered class is Read. This establishes descriptive model/state heterogeneity; it does not establish held-out stability, useful pruning or accuracy retention.

Existing force-off interventions suppress routes and do not free expert weights. Useful physical expert removal therefore needs new engineering and paired inference on an untouched evaluation population, but new generation is unnecessary merely to recompute benchmark scores, class profiles, frequency ranks or the already completed B4/anticipation analyses. Runtime measurements must account for repacking, shared scales, shared experts, draft paths, allocator reservations and KV/workspace allocations. Parameter masks alone cannot demonstrate the user's resident-memory objective.

## Exact method and figure audit for class-expert usage associations

The durable receipt is `EXPERT_ASSOCIATION_METHOD-r1.json`. It binds five original estimator files and seven A-cohort provenance files; all **35/35 source-hash comparisons pass**. The complete 49-cell heatmap selects `pooled / cls_expert / usage_all / mean / lendiff_frac` from the 147-row saved mean-usage export. All 49 model/class cells, original numeric values and the figure receipt/output hashes match; no sign or significance filter is used. The PNG was visually checked. The paired PDF hash was verified, without a separate PDF rendering in this audit.

Each cell is partial Pearson correlation with adjudicated binary correctness. Mean usage is selected-slot share of the class-enriched expert set over **all reasoning tokens**, averaged equally over layers containing at least one selected expert. Sets are selected outcome-blind on the full cohort: within-class enrichment q<0.05, positive log odds, normalized slot-share difference at least 0.01, top three per layer. Controls are intercept, dataset fixed effects, log completion tokens, retrospective question difficulty, the class token fraction **among labelled tokens**, and a capped-output indicator when present. Capped attempts remain included; GPT and Qwen3.6 have no A-cohort caps. Difficulty excludes the current outcome but uses the other same-model attempts on repeated benchmarks or other-model outcomes on single-attempt benchmarks, so it is retrospective.

Original intervals use 1,000 **question-ID** cluster bootstrap resamples with nuisance coefficients refitted. They are not duplicate-family bootstrap intervals, and the selected expert sets and difficulty features remain fixed. P-values come from analytic partial-correlation t-tests. Saved BH q-values cover **21 mean-usage tests per model** (seven classes times all/inside/outside usage) within pooled/cls_expert/summary/lendiff_frac; they do not correct jointly across the 49 displayed cells. Seven displayed cells have original q<0.05. These same-cohort associations do not establish held-out usefulness, causal specialization, safe deletion or resident-memory benefit.
