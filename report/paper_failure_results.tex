\subsection{Qualitative inspection of paper-style failures}
\label{sec:paper-failure-inspection}

A reproducible, assistant-assisted excerpt inspection examines 22 saved
historically baseline-correct/guided-incorrect pairs from strength-$+1$ paper-style
promotion. For each complete GPT-OSS polarity, four cases are sampled
without replacement from outputs ending at the token limit and four from
outputs ending normally. Six additional capped cases are sampled from the
interrupted Qwen positive run. Sampling uses sorted attempt IDs and seed
20260924, with separate streams per condition and termination stratum.
The OSS samples cover eight and seven distinct problems; the Qwen sample
covers four. Selection conditions on recorded correctness loss and
termination, and repeated attempts can share a problem. The resulting
counts describe this sample, not the prevalence of failure modes.

The review examines fixed beginning, middle, and ending excerpts, together
with the most frequent exact 16-word span, against the corresponding
baseline answer. Repetition is described from the text, not inferred from
output length alone. The whole-text diagnostic counts case-sensitive,
whitespace-delimited spans, allowing overlap; it does not capture every
form of repetition, particularly unspaced symbol chains. This is neither
independent human annotation nor exhaustive verification of the derivations.

\paragraph{Repetition and termination.}
All 14 sampled capped outputs contain repeated arithmetic or verbal
cycles and lack a boxed-answer marker. Five of the eight normally ended
OSS outputs also contain cycles before returning an incorrect boxed
answer. Across these 19 repetitive outputs, the most frequent exact
16-word span occurs 62--9,906 times; the matched baseline maxima range
from one to four. For example, positive OSS case P03 repeats
\texttt{390?} while its baseline returns 49. Qwen case Q06 repeatedly
emits ``Wait, wait.'' while its baseline returns 82. Negative OSS case
N06 repeats an unresolved sum and eventually returns 112 instead of 144.
Thus repetition can accompany both failure to finish and a normally
terminated wrong answer. The separate non-cyclic arithmetic example N05
correctly enumerates three, two, and two choices but evaluates
$3\times2\times2=6$, returning 6 instead of 12.

\paragraph{Scoring disagreement.}
Two normally ended positive OSS cases expose formatting sensitivity in
the saved fallback scorer. Case P05 returns \texttt{14/15}, equivalent
to the gold $\frac{14}{15}$; case P08 returns \texttt{East}, equivalent
to the gold \verb|\text{east}|. Both are stored as incorrect, whereas
their baselines use the gold LaTeX representation and are stored as correct.
The recorded method is \texttt{normalized\_exact\_numeric\_fallback};
its normalized-string or floating-point comparison rejects these pairs.
The classification concerns final-answer equivalence, not the validity
of every intermediate step. The subsequent offline audit in
Section~\ref{sec:guiding-rescoring} corrects both examples and applies the
same rule to all saved baseline and guided evaluations. Current accuracy
and bootstrap tables use those revised decisions. This qualitative sample
retains its original IDs and selection based on historical judgments;
P05 and P08 are consequently no longer correctness losses under the
revised rule. Resampling after correction would answer a different
qualitative question.

\paragraph{Scope of interpretation.}
The review supplies direct text examples of repetition, incorrect arithmetic,
and scoring artefacts. It does not isolate why the routing intervention
causes a particular trajectory, quantify population-level looping, or
extend the interrupted Qwen result to its missing attempts. Full-population
termination counts remain those in the generation-diagnostic tables.
The sampled IDs, source hashes, excerpts, text diagnostics, and case-level
notes are stored in \path{report/paper_failure_inspection.json} and
\path{report/paper_failure_review.json}.
