\section{Qwen Correlation Pipeline}

\subsection{Statistical measures and literature basis}
\label{sec:qwen-corr-metrics}

The corrected analysis selects the association measure from the scale of the
outcome and the unit of analysis, as summarized in
Table~\ref{tab:qwen-corr-metrics}.  This specification applies to the corrected
run.  The numerical tables later in this section document the previous run and
will be regenerated only after generation and routing replay pass the
truncation and completeness audits.

\begin{table*}[t]
\centering
\small
\caption{Prespecified measures for the expanded correlation analysis.}
\label{tab:qwen-corr-metrics}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.20\textwidth}p{0.14\textwidth}p{0.18\textwidth}p{0.39\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule

\midrule

& Generation trace
& Point-biserial $r_{\mathrm{pb}}$
& The predictor is continuous and the outcome is binary; the signed
coefficient is exactly Pearson's $r$ after coding the outcome as 0/1. \\

Mean feature versus avg@$N$ correctness
& Source problem
& Spearman's $\rho$
& Avg@$N$ is bounded, often tied, and not assumed to have a linear interval
relationship with a feature.  Ranking tests monotone association and one row
per problem avoids treating repeated attempts as independent. \\

Continuous feature--feature and continuous expert-rate associations
& Trace or source problem
& Spearman's $\rho$
& These exploratory scans ask whether quantities vary monotonically without
assuming comparable scales or a linear relationship. \\

Correct-versus-incorrect feature difference within a repeated problem
& Mixed-outcome source problem
& Mean paired contrast
& This directly controls for the problem by forming one correct-minus-incorrect
contrast per problem; uncertainty is obtained by resampling problems. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table*}

For a continuous feature $X$ and binary target $Y\in\{0,1\}$, the first
coefficient is
\begin{equation}
  r_{\mathrm{pb}} = \operatorname{Corr}(X,Y).
\end{equation}
Thus positive values mean that the feature tends to be higher for correct (or
event-present) traces, and negative values mean that it tends to be lower.
Liu et al. directly use point-biserial correlation for continuous language-model
quantities against binary answer correctness, making it the closest precedent
for this case~\cite{liu-etal-2022-figurative}.

For repeated-attempt datasets, correctness and each predictor are first
aggregated once per source problem.  Only groups containing exactly the
declared avg@$N$ unique sample identifiers, no duplicate generations detected
by the saved fingerprints, and a complete set of binary outcomes enter the
primary Spearman analysis.  Spearman correlation is appropriate here because
its target is monotone rank association rather than a specifically linear
change in avg@$N$.  Hong et al. correlate a
scalar projection of residual-stream states with graded reasoning scores using
Spearman correlation~\cite{hong-etal-2025-interplay}; Xu et al. use Spearman
correlation between language-model-derived conceptual quantities and human
ratings~\cite{xu-etal-2025-conceptual}; and Hewitt and Manning use it to compare
geometry derived from contextual representations with gold syntactic
structure~\cite{hewitt-manning-2019-structural}.  These studies are analogous
precedents for rank correlation with representation-derived quantities; they
do not by themselves establish Spearman as a unique choice for avg@32.
Zhang et al. additionally report Pearson and Spearman correlations together
when relating model uncertainty, including entropy-based measures, to graded
human hallucination judgments~\cite{zhang-etal-2023-uncertainty}.

Uncertainty is reported with 95\% percentile bootstrap intervals that resample
whole source problems, keeping attempts from the same problem together.  Xu et
al. provide relevant precedent for bootstrap intervals and use 10,000
resamples in one category--feature analysis~\cite{xu-etal-2025-conceptual}; the
resample count in this pipeline remains explicit and configurable rather than
being justified by that number alone.  Benjamini--Hochberg $q$-values are
computed separately within each declared dataset/scope, target, aggregation,
or cross-feature family.  Naive trace-level $p$-values are retained only as
diagnostics when a dataset contains repeated attempts, and layerwise and
expert-identity screens remain exploratory.

\subsection{Seven-benchmark run}

The September 2, 2026 run completed generation, teacher-forced routing replay,
and cluster-aware analysis for seven of the eight default SPIRAL benchmarks.
It contains 6,627 scored traces from 1,745 source problems.  MMLU-Pro, the last
benchmark in the default order, was not run and is omitted from every total and
association below.  The run uses one attempt per problem on MATH-500,
OlympiadBench, and Minerva Math; 32 attempts per problem on AIME 2024, AIME
2025, and AMC 2023; and ten attempts with deterministically permuted answer
choices on GPQA-Diamond.

Generation uses Qwen3.5-35B-A3B at temperature 0.6 and top-$p$ 0.95 through
the \texttt{UD-Q4\_K\_XL} GGUF with its embedded native-MTP weights.  The
saved messages are replayed through the matching Hugging Face checkpoint with
runtime 4-bit quantization.  Compact features are computed at the six router
layers selected independently by \texttt{probeTest}: 27, 34, 35, 36, 38, and
39.  The analysis exposes 83 features and uses 500 problem-cluster bootstrap
replicates for the prespecified aggregate features.

\subsection{Trace-level correctness associations}

Table~\ref{tab:qwen-corr-correct} reports the prespecified marginal
point-biserial associations.  The most consistent result is response length:
correct traces are shorter on all seven datasets, with $r$ from $-0.356$ to
$-0.690$ and all problem-cluster intervals excluding zero.  Mean normalized
router confidence is positively associated with correctness on six datasets
($r=0.224$--$0.609$); Minerva is the exception ($r=0.036$, 95\% CI
$[-0.081,0.154]$).  Reduced hidden-state step distance also has a negative
association on six datasets, while GPQA-Diamond is null.  Hidden-state norm is
negative on the five mathematics datasets other than Minerva, but null on
Minerva and GPQA-Diamond.

The broader run therefore revises the earlier preliminary interpretation.
Router confidence is no longer a MATH-500-only result: its positive direction
appears in six benchmarks and is nonzero under the cluster bootstrap in each
of those six.  It is still not universal.  Minerva confidence is null, and its
top-1/top-2 margin reverses direction.  Transition metrics are also
heterogeneous: correct MATH-500, AMC, and Olympiad traces generally switch
experts less, whereas Minerva and GPQA show more switching and less adjacent
top-$k$ overlap.  A single dataset-pooled coefficient would conceal these
differences and is not treated as replication evidence.

\subsection{Repeated-attempt analyses}

The repeated designs now make comparisons within the same question possible.
There are both correct and incorrect attempts for 19 of 30 AIME 2024 problems,
15 of 30 AIME 2025 problems, and 27 of 40 AMC 2023 problems.  In all three
datasets, the mean correct attempt is shorter and has slightly higher router
confidence and top-1/top-2 margin than the mean incorrect attempt; all nine
problem-bootstrap intervals in Table~\ref{tab:qwen-corr-within} exclude zero.
Thus, differences in which problems were sampled do not by themselves explain
these three effects.

Problem-level results agree with this pattern but remain exploratory.  Mean
token count has Spearman $\rho=-0.606$, $-0.747$, and $-0.845$ with per-problem
pass rate on AIME 2024, AIME 2025, and AMC 2023, respectively.  Mean router
confidence has $\rho=0.228$, $0.569$, and $0.742$.  On GPQA-Diamond, for which
within-problem contrasts are excluded, the corresponding problem-level values
are $-0.789$ and $0.360$.  The analyzer retains naive problem-level $p$-values
only as diagnostics and does not supply multiplicity-adjusted uncertainty for
these rankings.

The within-problem result controls source-question identity, but it does not
show that routing concentration contributes information beyond trace length or
token composition.  Correct attempts are hundreds of tokens shorter in the
same comparisons.  A length-conditioned, dataset-aware model is therefore
needed before calling confidence an independent predictor, and observational
correlation cannot identify a causal routing mechanism.

\subsection{Lexical events and storage audit}

Backtracking and self-correction markers are primarily markers of longer
traces.  In the pooled data their correlations with token count are $0.425$
and $0.332$, and with router confidence are $-0.256$ and $-0.205$.  The length
direction recurs dataset by dataset, but the confidence association is null on
both AIME datasets and on Minerva.  Contradiction is weaker and less stable.
The event rates in Table~\ref{tab:qwen-corr-data} also vary sharply across
datasets.  These lexical detectors are descriptive trace-style indicators,
not validated reasoning-episode annotations, and pooled event correlations
partly encode benchmark identity.

The compact extraction design succeeds operationally at the larger scale.
Across 49.93 million retained tokens, full selected-layer payloads would have
required 1.534 TB (1.395 TiB).  The run instead stores 4.81 GB of compact
tensor artifacts and 5.30 GB of per-dataset files, while recording
\texttt{raw\_tensors\_saved: false} and
\texttt{expert\_weights\_saved: false}.  Compact tensor storage is about
0.31\% of the projected raw payload, avoiding more than 99.68\% while
preserving the analyzed scalar features and top-$k$ expert identifiers.

The immediate remaining execution task is MMLU-Pro.  The principal analysis
task is a prespecified model that conditions on length and benchmark while
respecting problem clusters; layerwise maxima should remain exploratory unless
their selection and multiplicity are handled explicitly.
